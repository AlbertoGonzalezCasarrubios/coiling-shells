# Changelog

All notable changes to this project are documented in this file. The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and the project uses [semantic versioning](https://semver.org/).

## [1.2.0] - 2026-11-10

### Added
- The page address also stores the look: line, shell and background colours (once chosen), mesh resolution, growth bands and axis, so a shared link opens with the same appearance.
- Undo includes the look: a change of colour, palette, resolution, bands or axis is a step like any other.
- Spines on bivalves. Spines used to be left out of the first whorl, so a bivalve, with a single whorl, could never have them; now they start after the first whorls or, if that comes earlier, where the shell reaches 20 % of its final size. Coiled shells are unchanged. New example: the spiny venus *Pitar lupanaria*.
- *Naticarius* pattern preset: spots in spiral rows. A medium-range inhibitor keeps the activator peaks apart, as in the bands, and outlives the activator, as in the flames, so each peak switches on and off in place.
- The 3D printing section warns, only when the shell has spines, if the largest ones would be too thin or slender to print well at the chosen size and wall.

### Changed
- The pattern follows a dragged slider: a new simulation starts as soon as the previous one ends, with the latest values, instead of waiting until the slider is released.
- The *Save pattern* button of the pigment tab offers PNG, JPG or TIFF, the same choice as in the export window.
- Secondary sections of the sidebar fold: the pigment colours and, in the ornament tab, ribs, cords, spines, keel and change with growth, which now have equal rank. The ornament sections the shell has open by themselves when an example is loaded, and the rest stay folded.
- The sidebar's scrollbar is thin, in the theme colours, and only visible while the pointer is over the panel.

- jsPDF updated from 2.5.1 to 4.2.1. Version 2.5.1 has published security advisories; the app was not exposed to them in practice, because it only writes its own images and text into the PDF, but the update removes them.
- Development: ESLint and Prettier (`npm run lint`, also in CI); the code is formatted with Prettier. The browser test serves Three.js, MathJax and jsPDF from local copies installed with `npm ci` at the same versions as the CDN, so it no longer depends on the CDN, and a test keeps those versions equal.

### Fixed
- Since the code was organised in folders (1.1.0) the pattern worker was looked for in the wrong folder: patterns were computed on the main thread, which froze the interface for a moment on every change, and the thumbnails of the pattern gallery stayed blank. The browser test now checks both.

## [1.1.0] - 2026-11-09

### Added
- Welcome guide on the first visit: an interactive 3D snail generated by the model, with a reticulate relief and spines added and its parts labelled (apex, spire, suture, body whorl, aperture, coiling axis, rib, cord and spine), and a five-step tutorial whose last step presents the shell creator. The *Guide* button opens it again and `?guide` in the address always shows it; it does not open by itself from shared links.
- Language selector in the guide.
- *Create your own shell*: a window that walks through five steps (shape, ornament, colour, look and export) with simplified controls and plain-language names for Raup's parameters, around a live 3D view. It is the app itself, not a copy: its 3D canvas moves into the window and every control acts on the same state, so undo, the page address and all the exports work as usual. The colour step offers solid colour, the pattern presets and the oblique waves of the activator–substrate model, with the simple sliders of the pigment tab and a button for another variant. The last step names the shell and downloads a wallpaper the size of the screen or the PDF sheet, or opens the export window.
- Image export in JPG and TIFF as well as PNG, for the current view, the plate of views and the space–time record.
- Fixed image sizes for the current view (16:9 Full HD, QHD and 4K, 16:10, 21:9, 4:3, 1:1, phone screens, the device's own screen, or a custom size), useful as wallpapers. The framing adapts to the new proportions so the shell is never cut.
- The plate of standard views can be exported at the same sizes; the six views are arranged in the grid (3 × 2, 2 × 3, 6 × 1 or 1 × 6) that makes them largest in the chosen format.
- A name of your own for the shell, used as the title of the PDF sheet and as the name of the downloaded files.
- PDF background: white, the shell images on the current colour, or the whole sheet in the current colour.
- *Peringia ulvae* shape preset.
- Undo: the arrow button in the toolbar, or Ctrl+Z / ⌘Z, goes back one step (shape, ornament, render mode and pattern; quick successive changes such as dragging a slider make one step).
- *Remove ornament* and *Remove colour* buttons.
- *Controls* page in the About window, with the mouse, touch and keyboard controls, which no longer take space over the 3D view.
- *Ornament* tab: a relief added to Raup's surface along the normal of the aperture, growing with the shell. Axial ribs (number, height, width and an asymmetry for varices), spiral cords, nodules where they cross, a keel, spines in one or two rows, and a change of strength with growth. Its examples load the shape of a real shell together with its relief: *Epitonium clathrus*, *Turritella*, *Calliostoma*, *Cancellaria*, *Cerithium*, the sun shell *Astraea*, a ribbed and a spiny ammonite, and a cockle; shells that Raup's model cannot draw, such as *Murex* with its siphonal canal, are left out. The relief appears in the equation, the 3D files and the PDF sheet, which describes it in words under the title and lists its values with the parameters.
- With ornament, the line drawing follows the relief, like a scientific illustration: growth lines on the crests of the ribs, spiral lines on the crests of the cords and keel, and each spine outlined as a cone.
- Mechanical model of the spiral cords, after Moulton, Goriely and Chirat: the mantle edge as an elastic rod on an elastic foundation, which buckles when it grows faster than the margin. An interactive panel draws the buckling load against the number of waves and the buckled margin, and grows the predicted cords on the shell. A new About page, *Ornament · mechanics*, explains the descriptive and the mechanical models with their references.
- Orientation of the shell in the viewer: coiling axis vertical (orbit around it, as before), horizontal (planispiral shells such as *Nautilus* and ammonites seen face-on, chosen automatically by their presets) or free rotation, turning the shell by hand in any direction. It only changes the presentation: the plate of views, the GLB, STL and 3MF files keep the model frame, while the image of the current view shows what is on screen.

### Fixed
- The PNG export at four times the screen resolution was cropped on high-density screens, because the browser granted a smaller drawing buffer than requested. Images of the current view are now drawn in tiles, so their size no longer depends on the drawing buffer.

### Changed
- The code is organised in folders by role: `js/model/` (the science, pure and tested), `js/export/` (file formats), `js/ui/` (one module per part of the interface, connected by `main.js`), `js/i18n/` (interface strings, About pages and formulas in separate files) and `js/workers/`. `main.js` went from about 3 800 to 600 lines. Nothing changes for the user.
- A test drives the whole interface in headless Chrome (guide, tabs, modes, orientations, equations, undo, shell creator, About pages and exports) and fails on any error in the page; `npm run test:fast` skips it.
- The ornament tab sits between the shape and pigment tabs, and has simple controls (number and height of ribs, cords and spines) and advanced ones (widths, asymmetry, positions, keel, change with growth and the mechanical model). Spines are drawn whole: never across the lip of the aperture, where they would open it into a funnel, nor on the first whorls.
- *Appearance* (colours, background, resolution, lines, axis) moved from the shape tab to a menu in the viewer toolbar, because it affects every tab.
- The app remembers the visitor's choice of language (it still opens in English the first time).
- *Trochus* preset: lower expansion rate (W = 1.45), more whorls (9) and a flatter aperture, for the many-whorled, straight-sided cone of real top shells.
- *Physa* preset: lower expansion rate (W = 2.6) and whorls coiled tightly around the axis (D = 0), with a short pointed spire over a large body whorl.
- *Nautilus* pattern preset: faster reactions, stronger saturation and weaker coupling between cells give about ten broad, wavy flame stripes per whorl instead of three thin lines.
- Changing to the activator–substrate model now loads an excitable set of constants that draws travelling waves (oblique lines and V shapes).

### Removed
- *Oliva porphyria* pattern preset. The simplified global control of the app gives oblique lines and V shapes but not the dense network of triangles of a real *Oliva*, so the preset did not look like the species. The model remains available in the advanced controls, and the explanations now say what it can and cannot reproduce.

## [1.0.0] - 2026-11-06

First release in its own repository. The app was previously published as `shells-morphology/` in [aplicaciones-varias](https://github.com/AlbertoGonzalezCasarrubios/aplicaciones-varias); that address now redirects here.

### Shape
- Raup's coiling model with W, D, T, S, N and dextral or sinistral coiling, with the equation shown in general form and with the current values.
- Mirror valve for equivalve bivalves, and presets for gastropods, cephalopods and a bivalve.
- Line, solid and combined render modes; the six standard views of malacology; growth animation.

### Morphospace
- Interactive W–D morphospace with the contact curve D = 1/W and the approximate regions of the main groups, drawn as a section of Raup's W–D–T cube at the current T.
- Group names load a typical shell of each group; the W axis can span 1–10 or 1–10⁴.

### Colour
- Meinhardt's activator–inhibitor and activator–substrate models, with global control and cell insertion.
- Presets for *Nautilus*, *Cepaea nemoralis* and *Oliva porphyria*; simple and advanced controls.
- Space–time record in time or unrolled with the true proportions of the shell; margin profile of the activator and the inhibitor or substrate.
- Pattern cells spaced by arc length along elliptical apertures.

### Export
- PNG of the current view, plate of standard views, PDF sheet with optional equations, closed solids for 3D printing (STL, 3MF) and a coloured 3D model (GLB).

### Project
- English and Spanish interface, explanations and documentation.
- Automated test suite (`npm test`) with continuous integration.
